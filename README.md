# Stud-Anchored Workbench with Lower Shelf

**Beginner's build guide** — 60"W × 24"D × 36⅛"H, glue-free

A wall-anchored garage workbench designed for unfinished stud walls. Bolt the back to your framing, brace the legs with kickers, and you get a stiff bench without store-bought prices — and without glue.

**[Open the interactive 3D build guide →](https://dpappo.github.io/workbench/)**

The guide walks through all 15 build steps in 3D (orbit, zoom, keyboard navigation) and includes cut-plan diagrams. This README is the printable reference.

## How this bench works

Most workbenches are freestanding tables: four legs, with all the stability coming from the joints. This design bolts the back directly to the wooden skeleton of your garage wall — the vertical studs behind unfinished drywall or bare framing. Once bolted on, the bench can't wobble without moving the building. That's why it out-stiffens many store-bought benches at twice the price.

### Five groups of parts, five jobs

| Part | Job |
|------|-----|
| **Ledger** | Horizontal 2×4 bolted flat against the studs; carries the back edge of the top |
| **Front frame** | Two legs and a horizontal beam (like a soccer goal); carries the front edge |
| **Joists** | Six short 2×4s bridging ledger to beam; the top rests on them |
| **Kickers** | Two short boards low down, tying each leg back to the wall; also carry the shelf's front rail |
| **Top** | Two ¾" plywood layers screwed into one stiff 1½" slab, capped by ⅛" hardboard |

### Lumber sizes, decoded

- A **2×4** actually measures **1½" × 3½"** (the name is its rough size before milling).
- A **doubled 2×4** is therefore **3"** thick.
- **8-ft studs** are sometimes really **92⅝"** (wall-framing standard); this plan works either way. Every dimension below already accounts for this.

### Why two screw sizes?

- **2½" screws** join 1½"-thick wood to wood: through one board, an inch of bite into the next.
- **1¼" screws** attach sheets (ply-to-ply, hardboard, pegboard).

**Fastener rule:** A screw must pass completely through the first piece and bite at least ~1" into the second. Where the first piece is a doubled (3") member, a 2½" screw can't get through — those joints get **4" lag bolts** or **toe-screws** (screws driven at ~45° through the side of one board into the other).

**At the lumber rack:** Sight down each stud's long edge like a rifle barrel; reject any that curve ("bow") or corkscrew ("twist"). Ten minutes of picking saves the whole day.

## The cuts

**Terms:** A **crosscut** goes across a board; a **rip** runs along a sheet's length. Your blade eats ~⅛" per cut (the **kerf**) — this plan budgets every one.

### 2×4 cuts

| Cut | Qty | Becomes |
|-----|-----|---------|
| 2×4 × 60" | 6 | Ledger, beam (×2), shelf cleat, shelf rail (×2) |
| 2×4 × 34½" | 4 | Legs (2 doubled) |
| 2×4 × 19½" | 6 | Top joists |
| 2×4 × 21" | 2 | Kickers |
| 2×4 × 16" | 2 | Kicker wall blocks |
| 2×4 × 10½" | 3 | Shelf joists |
| 2×4 × ~8½" (cut to fit in place) | 1 | Optional centre post under shelf rail |

### Sheet cuts

| Material | Cut | Qty | Becomes |
|----------|-----|-----|---------|
| ¾" ply | 23⅞" × 60" | 1 | Top, bottom layer |
| ¾" ply | 24" × 60" | 1 | Top, top layer |
| ¾" ply | 15" × 35⅞" + 15" × 24⅛" | 2 | Shelf deck (butt together into 15" × 60") |
| ⅛" hardboard | 24" × 48" + 23⅞" × 12" | 2 | Wear surface (48 + 12 = 60) |

### Why pieces come out ⅛" narrow

One rip through a 48"-wide panel can't make two 24" strips — the kerf steals ⅛". For the plywood top: the narrow strip hides on the bottom against the wall; the full 24" strip goes on top, flush with the bench front. The hardboard's second strip is 23⅞" wide — install it with the ⅛" shortfall against the wall, where it disappears.

If plywood runs scant (47⅞" instead of 48"), both top strips come out 23⅞", the top layer sits ⅛" shy of the beam face, and nothing else changes.

### Lumber cutting order

Offcuts feed the next cuts — verified to work even with 92⅝" studs:

1. **Studs 1–6:** one 60" piece each. Keep the ~36" offcuts.
2. **From each of those six offcuts:** one 19½" top joist.
3. **Studs 7–8:** two 34½" legs each. Keep the offcuts.
4. **From stud 7's offcut:** two 10½" shelf joists. From stud 8's offcut: the third.
5. **Stud 9:** two 21" kickers, two 16" wall blocks, and the optional centre post — cut the post last, to the gap you measure in place (Step 8).
6. **Stud 10** stays whole — insurance against a miscut or hidden flaw.

Measure each offcut before cutting from it.

### Plywood sequence

1. One full-length rip at 23⅞" → strips of 23⅞" and 24"
2. Crosscut each strip at 60" → the two top layers
3. From the 24"-wide leftover, rip at 15" → shelf piece 15 × 35⅞
4. From the 23⅞"-wide leftover, rip at 15", crosscut at 24⅛" → shelf piece 15 × 24⅛

### Hardboard sequence

Rip the 48×48 panel at 24" → strips of 24" and 23⅞"; crosscut the 23⅞" strip at 12". The leftover 23⅞ × 35⅞ piece is your future replacement blank.

### Making a straight cut

Measure, mark a small V, draw the line through it with the rafter square. Support the board so the offcut can fall free — never support both sides of a cut, or the closing gap pinches the blade. Set blade depth to poke ~¼" below the wood. Cut on the **waste side** of the line. For long plywood rips, clamp or screw a straight 2×4 to the sheet as a guide rail.

## Step-by-step

### Step 1 — Draw layout lines on the wall

Two horizontal lines across the studs: **34½"** (ledger top) and **12"** (cleat top). Don't measure up from the floor at each stud — floors slope. Measure once at one stud, hold the level horizontal at the mark, centre the bubble, draw along its edge across all studs in your 5-ft span. Repeat at 12".

### Step 2 — Lag the ledger

Hold a 60" 2×4 with its top on the 34½" line. Drill **7/32"** pilot holes through the ledger and ~2½" into the stud — two holes per stud (one high, one low), at four studs. Washer on each lag; drive until the washer presses firmly into the wood, then stop.

### Step 3 — Screw the shelf cleat to the studs

A 60" 2×4 flat against the wall on the 12" line, two 2½" screws per stud. Projects 1½" — carries the back of the shelf.

### Step 4 — Make the doubled parts

The beam, shelf rail, and each leg are two 2×4s face-screwed into one 3"-thick member. Stack each pair flush, drive 2½" screws every 8" in a zigzag (near top edge, near bottom edge, alternating). Make: one beam (2× 60"), one rail (2× 60"), two legs (2 pairs × 34½").

### Step 5 — Build the front frame on the floor

Lay doubled legs parallel, 60" apart outside-to-outside, wide (3½") faces up. Lay the beam across their top ends wide face down, flush at both corners. Check corners with the rafter square. Drive two **4" lag bolts** down through the beam into each leg (7/32" pilot, washers): through 3" of beam, 1" bite into the leg.

### Step 6 — Stand and set the frame

Stand the frame so the gap between ledger front face and beam back face is exactly **19½"** — a cut joist held in the gap at each end is the perfect spacer. Level from ledger to beam; trim a leg or shim if needed. Check square: measure diagonals from ledger ends to opposite beam ends — equal numbers = square. Working alone, screw a scrap 2×4 diagonally from beam to a wall stud as a temporary brace.

### Step 7 — Tie the legs to the wall with kickers

Before fastening, check where each kicker's tail lands. Stud there? No block needed. Bare bay? Install a **16" wall block** flat against the wall at kicker height, two 2½" screws into each of the two nearest studs. Set kicker height from the level (not the floor): hold level across from the cleat's bottom edge and mark the leg. Screw kicker to leg with three 2½" screws, then fasten tail with two **toe-screws** (~45° through the board's side). Start straight-in for a few turns, then tilt and drive.

### Step 8 — Set the shelf rail

Rest the doubled rail across the two kickers; top of rail at 12" (check with level). Flush ends with frame outside edges; toe-screw down into each kicker, two screws per end. Optional centre post: measure actual gap from floor to rail underside at midpoint; cut post to fit; toe-screw top and bottom.

### Step 9 — Install the top joists

Six 19½" joists on edge between ledger and beam, tops flush. Outer joists ~4" in from bench ends; space the rest evenly (no gap wider than ~12"). Fasten each end with two 2½" toe-screws. Re-measure diagonals as confirmation.

### Step 10 — Frame and deck the shelf

Three 10½" shelf joists flat between cleat and rail back face, tops flush at 12": one near each end, third at 35⅞" from the left (under the deck seam). Two toe-screws per end. Drop on the 15 × 35⅞ and 15 × 24⅛ ply pieces; screw with 1¼" screws every ~8".

### Step 11 — Top, bottom layer

The 23⅞" × 60" ply goes down first, flush to the wall; front edge ⅛" shy of the beam face. Screw with 2½" screws, countersunk: two per joist, plus one every ~12" along ledger and beam edges. Heads must sit below flush so the next layer lies flat.

### Step 12 — Top, top layer

The 24" × 60" sheet, front edge flush with the beam face. Screw to the layer below with 1¼" screws on an ~8" grid, countersunk, working centre-outward. Stay a couple inches off Step 11's screw lines.

### Step 13 — The wear surface

Hardboard pieces (24 × 48 and 23⅞ × 12), smooth side up. Narrow piece's ⅛" shortfall against the wall. ~4 screws per piece at corners/edges, 1¼", countersunk dead flush. No glue — back out screws and replace with the leftover blank or a fresh $11 panel when worn.

### Step 14 — Pegboard

Cut to 60" wide × whatever height fills the space above the bench. Screw straight to the studs, 1¼" screws every 12". Open stud bays provide the hook gap for free — no spacer strips needed.

### Step 15 — Finish

Ease front edge and shelf edges with 120-grit sandpaper. Done — not a drop of glue.

## Before you build — seating check

Sit on a stool at a kitchen counter (same height) with a box slid 9" back underneath at shin height.

- Want more room? **Narrow the shelf to 12"** (shelf joists become 7½")
- Or **raise the cleat/rail/kicker lines 4"** (the cut-to-fit post simply gets measured longer)

---

## Golden rule

Measure twice, mark with a V (a lone tick wanders), cut on the waste side, and check square or level before driving screws — screws are patient.
